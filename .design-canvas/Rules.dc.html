The rules — numbered because they are ordered by how often they get broken

What to check before shipping a screen

01

Red is never a routine button

Ink is the action colour. Signal red is the correction mark — the wordmark's ?, an error, a struck word. If a red button appears in this product, something is wrong; that is the whole rule, and it is what lets one value serve both the brand and the error state.

02

Chrome follows the reader; content follows the work

Launcher surfaces are Korean. The console stays English because its content is English — the interpreter is reading the next line they have to say, at speed. What is forbidden is mixing on one screen, which is what the old launcher did: Korean brand, Korean nav, English labels, English options, English CTA.

03

State is never colour alone

Always a word, a shape or a fill as well. The readers here include a colour-blind interpreter in a dark booth and a visitor at a counter who shares no language with the staff — for both, a red line and nothing else carries no information at all.

04

Brand devices are banned from working UI

Struck text, annotation underlines, the ≤3px nudge and the subtitle block belong on hero, onboarding, empty, success and illustration. Never in a live reading area or a form somebody is completing. A device that makes a task harder comes out — the concept survives that fine.

05

Jokes are allowed in empty and success. Nowhere else.

Not in errors, payment, privacy, permissions, or any decision the user cannot undo. <StateBlock> enforces this structurally: the error tone is a different rendering, not different copy, so you cannot ship a shrug on an error screen by accident.

06

44px minimum target, every size, including sm

These controls are pressed one-handed, in a hurry, often in low light, sometimes ninety seconds before a service starts. A small button is not a density decision here.

07

No component contains a raw colour

Everything resolves through a working token that a surface rebinds, which is what makes a component correct on paper and on the console without knowing which it is on. It is also what would make a future TDS mapping a token-layer change rather than a rewrite.

08

Korean text never takes Latin tracking

CSS cannot track by script. The 0.08em that makes a Latin caption look considered opens visible holes between Hangul syllables and reads as broken kerning. Captions here use 0.02em and let the mono face carry the voice.

09

Check it in a browser, not in Figma

Figma has no Korean font fallback chain, no --font-scale, and no dual-axis type sizing. Every Korean line break, every console type size and anything that depends on viewport height is indicative there and exact only in the product. See docs/design/figma-code-differences.md.

The test the whole thing has to pass: someone opening a screen should think “아, 여기서 뭘 하면 되는지 알겠다” first, and “근데 이거 은근 웃기네” a few seconds later. In that order. If the second one arrives first, the brand is in the way and something on the screen has to go.
